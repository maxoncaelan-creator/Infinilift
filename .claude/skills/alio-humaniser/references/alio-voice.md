## Who's talking

**A friendly local who knows Google inside out and explains it in plain English.**

Alio! Brand Studios helps local Australian businesses get more calls and bookings from Google, through fast websites, Google Business Profile and Google Ads tracked to real enquiries. One person does the work from start to finish, so first person ("I") is often more honest than "our team".

Personality, in order: **energetic and modern**, **bold and cheeky**, **friendly and down-to-earth**.

Plain English with no slang. Don't add Aussie colloquialisms ("mate", "arvo", "no worries") unless the user asks.

## Four principles

1. **Plain English first.** Short words, short sentences. If a busy owner can't understand it in one read, rewrite it.
2. **Lead with the result.** Calls, enquiries and bookings. Not impressions, reach or "visibility".
3. **Honest, always.** No guarantees about rankings. Say what we control, what we don't, and what we'll do next.
4. **Warm, with a wink.** A light joke when things go well. Never at the client's expense, never when something has gone wrong.

## We are, we're not

| We are | We're not |
|---|---|
| Friendly: "Hi Sam, here's your March report." | Over-familiar: "Heyyy legend!!" |
| Confident: "This will get you more calls." | Cocky: "We're the best agency in Australia." |
| Cheeky: "You have 12 reviews. They have 40. Let's close that gap." | Sarcastic: "Nice website… from 2009." |
| Clear: "People who found you on Google Maps." | Jargon-heavy: "Local pack SERP impressions." |
| Energetic: "Let's get your profile sorted this week." | Pushy: "Only 2 spots left! Book NOW!" |

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Website and social | Upbeat. Cheeky is welcome. | "Your Google profile is your new shopfront. Is yours open?" |
| Free audit | Helpful and specific. No hard sell. | "Three quick wins: add photos, reply to reviews, fix your hours." |
| Proposals | Clear and confident. Exact scope and prices. | "What's included, what isn't, and what it costs each month." |
| Monthly reports | Plain and honest. Results first, slow months included. | "Calls dipped 8% in June. Here's why, and what we're changing." |
| Outreach | Short, relevant, respectful. Say who you are; include an opt-out (Spam Act 2003). | "I recorded a 5-minute video on how you show up on Google. Want the link?" |
| Problems and complaints | Calm and direct. Own it. No jokes, no Ollie. | "We missed this. Here's what happened and how we'll fix it by Friday." |
| Contracts and policies | Plain, firm and fair. The rule first, then what happens. Caelan's own terms, "we" and no names. No Ollie, no jokes. | "You pay, then you get 30 days of service. Cancel any time and service ends when those 30 days run out." |
| Automatic emails (sign-in links, codes, password resets) | Short and polite. The action, the link and nothing else. Signed "Alio! Brand Studios", no name. No Ollie. | "Please tap the link below to reset the password for you@example.com on the Alio! client portal." |

## Writing rules

- **Australian English:** optimise, colour, enquiry, organisation, centre, analyse.
- **The name is "Alio!"**: capital A, always with the "!". Write "Alio! Brand Studios" on first mention in formal documents. Don't add a full stop straight after it. Never "ALIO" or "Alio" (URLs and handles excepted).
- **One exclamation mark per page**, not counting the name.
- **Sentence case** for headings and buttons. Buttons start with a verb: "Get my free audit".
- **Numbers as numerals:** 3 calls, 12%, $550 a month. Dates: 26 September 2026. Times: 9 am, 5:30 pm.
- **"You" and "we" (or "I")**, not "the client" and "the agency". The website, emails, reports and the portal say "I". Legal documents (contracts, the NDA, the freelancer agreement, the privacy policy and the website terms) say "we" and "Alio!", the way Caelan talks about the business ("we charge", "the property of Alio!"), and never name Caelan: "Alio! Brand Studios" and the ABN identify the business. Non-legal documents can say "I" and name Caelan. In a contract, write "each side" or "either side" for the two parties, so "we" only ever means Alio!.

## Promises (never contradict these)

- **You own everything.** Domain, Google profile and ad accounts stay in the client's name; Alio! gets manager access.
- **Reports you can read.** Every month: calls, enquiries and what's next. No jargon, no vanity charts.
- **No lock-in.** Monthly plans are paid 30 days at a time and can be cancelled whenever the client likes; the plan runs to the end of the 30 days they've paid for. (The brand guidelines PDF and its source still say "3-month minimum" until the next rebuild. Follow this file.)
- **One person, start to finish.** Clients deal with the person doing the work, so write in first person ("I") on the website and in emails.

## Ollie the frog (mascot)

Ollie is curious, upbeat, a bit cheeky and always helpful. Ollie can appear in social posts, the "Ollie's tip" box at the end of monthly reports, stickers and thank-you pages. Ollie stays out of invoices, contracts, complaint replies and anything made for a client's own brand.

- Ollie would say: "Found you three quick wins." "38 calls this month. Not bad at all."
- Ollie wouldn't say: "Your website is a disaster, lol." "Guaranteed #1 on Google!"

You own your accounts, get a plain-English report every month and deal with the person doing the work, start to finish.

## Caelan's own voice

Caelan Maxon owns Alio! and does the work. Infinilift's plan, docs and PR descriptions are written in Caelan's voice. The full file keeps the messages these patterns come from. They're left out here because this repo is public.

| Caelan does this | In copy |
|---|---|
| Starts with the point | First line is the decision, the fact or the ask |
| States the rule, then what happens | "You pay, then you get 30 days of service. Cancel any time and service ends when those 30 days run out." |
| Uses real terms without fuss | Keep the real term where readers will get it. Translate shorthand for clients |
| Pins a category down with an example | "custom assets, such as custom icons" |
| Is honest about what isn't known | Say what's checked and what isn't. Never cover a gap with confident wording |
| Gives a go-ahead with limits | Permissions and promises come with their limits attached |
| Works it out together | Invite a reply or a decision; don't announce |
| Short numbered answers, no padding | Short sentences. Lists only when the items are real and separate |
| No hype | No exclamation marks except in the name, no sales words |
| Says what the thing is, then who it's for | Open with what it is and name who uses it |
| Names the platforms and places | "Google, Facebook and Instagram". "Liverpool, Camden, Penrith and Parramatta" |
| Puts promises in short, flat sentences | State a promise once, plainly, and move on |
| Gives the instruction, not the backstory | Open with what the reader does. Don't narrate what happened first |
| Says "please" when asking | A plain "please" on a request is fine. No "kindly" or padding |
| Puts the key fact in the noun | "your one-time link", in place of a second sentence explaining it |
| Cuts lines that only reassure | Keep it to the action and the detail |
| Wants only the words needed to act | Plans and specs keep only what someone needs to build or test the thing |
